# The Seven Bells: story notes

`src/content/ys.ink` has one knot per bell (`ys_1` to `ys_7`). Each plays once its bell has rung (`design/gate.md`). The seventh's story ends the run.

**Source.** The scenes were drafted by an independent writer (a subagent briefed only on the setting and the seven bells, told to rave like an occult death-metal bard, and told not to design). Its raw text follows. The ink condenses each scene and turns its closing "things the covenant could do" into choices with effect tags. Following `process.md`, new systems are proposed from this raw text together with the designer, not by the writer.

## Unused ideas from the raw text

Candidates for new systems, to discuss with the designer:

- The hands all dream the same dream after Wormwood: a shared dream as a covenant-wide event.
- Sabine's heart stops for the hidden hour: a magus-specific cost of Darkness.
- The Knight "waiting for orders since 709": orders as a standing choice (raid, guard, dig) instead of a one-off.
- "Ring the first bell backwards and call the sea home": an alternative ending that undoes the great work.
- Bonus ravings: every eel in Christendom swimming to Mont-Dol; Hamon's report written in Breton in a hand not his; a calf with a bell for a heart; an eighth bell carved into a Merveille capital; bog-oak stumps sprouting acorns; a key-shaped crystal in every sack of salt; a child who walked out of the bay; fresh claw marks on Mont-Dol.

## Raw text

The writer's scenes, verbatim apart from headings:

### The first bell: the Bell of Scissy

Equinox low water. The bell tolls under the mud like a cow bellowing in a buried cellar, and Guillaume, knee-deep at his weir, drops his basket. The tide keeps going out, past the eel stakes, past the last salt-pan, past every mark his grandfather cut. Then the forest rises. Black bog-oak, thousands of trunks snapped at shoulder height, glistening like tar and steaming in the cold. Five hundred years of rot comes out in one breath: sulphur, leaf-mould, wet iron, old bread. Between the stumps lie the drowned of 709, brown as saddle-leather, fists still full of acorns and kindling. A quarry hand swears one of them turned its head toward Mont-Dol. Aldric stands on the hill with his molars aching as if he'd bitten tin. The Tide Pool has gone flat as a mirror and shows no sky. Sabine counts the stumps three times and gets three different numbers. Hervé says nothing. He is smiling.

- Run out among the stumps before the tide turns, hack the heart from the oldest oak, and carry it home dripping.
- Bury the drowned in Christian ground, and fetch Rogier before he hears of it from someone else.
- Muffle the second bell tonight, while the only witnesses are eels.

### The second bell: the Bell of Blood

The second bell rings at dawn like a slaughterhouse door slamming. The bay goes red. It starts at the mouth of the Couesnon and spreads, and it isn't rust or weed but the red of an opened artery, clotting at the tideline into a skin the gulls won't touch. Mullet float up belly-first by the thousand. The eels crawl out of the weirs and die writhing in the salt-pans. Perrine's brine boils pink and crusts into salt that tastes of pennies and weeping. Guillaume stands at the covenant gate with forty fishermen, all gaffs and gutting knives, his beard caked red, screaming that the sorcerers have bled the sea. Hamon the sergeant stands at the back of the mob. He isn't stopping them. He's counting faces. Across the bay the Merveille scaffolds hang empty while the monks kneel on the rock. Sabine plunges her hand into the Tide Pool and draws it out gloved to the elbow in blood, and it is warm. The sea has a pulse, and it beats in time with the hill.

- Open the gate and tell Guillaume the whole truth, then let him choose whether to put a gaff through you.
- Harvest the blood-salt, because every grain is vis now, and pay Perrine triple to keep the fires lit.
- Ride for Dol and confess to Rogier before Hamon's report reaches the bishop.

### The third bell: the Bell of Wormwood

At the third bell a star falls. Every soul from Cancale to Avranches sees it, a green-white seed dragging a tail of smoke across the whole sky. It doesn't hiss into the bay. It SINGS one long bent note and strikes the Regio Spring. The spring boils up bitter. Within a week every well from Dol to Pontorson is bitter. Milk sours in the udder, ale in the cask, and babies spit out their mothers' kisses. The hands drink anyway, and all eleven of them dream the same dream, of a street paved in blue glass under green water. Rogier preaches from the cathedral steps with a tongue gone green: "And the name of the star is called Wormwood!" Aldric tastes the spring, and his mouth fills with the names of every man ever drowned in this bay, in order. He cannot stop reciting them. Hervé claws through the steaming mud with bare hands and lifts out a lump of star-iron the size of a lamb's heart. It is still ringing.

- Forge the star into a clapper for the fourth bell, and damn whatever it costs.
- Drink the spring dry, all three of you, and carry the bitterness in your own guts so the villages don't have to.
- Bottle the wormwood water and sell it to the Merveille as holy water, because the monks are terrified and paying in silver.

### The fourth bell: the Bell of Darkness

The fourth bell rings at noon, and a third of the sun is bitten out clean, like a coin with a bite taken from it. The light that's left is brown and thick, like light through a jar of old honey. Then an hour is cut out of the day. The Merveille's bell strikes None and then Vespers with nothing in between. Perrine loses an hour and finds her palms skinned raw beside a fresh salt-pan nobody dug. Hamon wakes on the strand with his sword drawn and wet, and he doesn't know whose blood is on it. The covenant walks inside the stolen hour. Birds hang mid-wingbeat. The tide stops like a breath held in a drowning chest, and the sea is hard as slate, so the magi walk on it. Far out, where only mud should be, they see lights. Windows. In a tower beneath the bay, someone in Ys has kept a candle burning for seven hundred years, in a window that faces Mont-Dol. Sabine's heart stops for the whole hour and starts again when it ends. She tells no one.

- Walk out across the frozen sea to that candle and knock on the door.
- Use the stolen hour to lift the bishop's letters and Hamon's report from their saddlebags.
- Leave one hand behind inside the hour as a watchman, knowing he'll be alone in there and you'll never learn how long he aged.

### The fifth bell: the Bell of the Pit

The fifth bell doesn't ring. It CRACKS, and the Drowned Knight's Barrow splits with it, open like a loaf of bread, and smoke pours out as from a great furnace, darkening the sun. The Knight climbs out: seven feet of barnacled mail, a helm full of scuttling crabs, a sword gone green, and a face the sea has polished to pearl. He kneels toward the Archangel's footprint on the hill. Then he rises and turns to the covenant, and the whole marsh hears his armour grind. The locusts come after him. They are the size of men, crowned with gold that is really bladderwrack, with the faces of drowned sailors, the hair of women and the teeth of lions, and their wings roar like chariots. They don't touch the grass. They hunt anyone without the seal on their brow. Two salt-pan hands are stung, and they will scream for five months and seek death and not find it. Hervé kneels in the ash and understands that the Knight is waiting for orders. From him. He has been waiting since 709.

- Give the Knight his first order: take the Merveille.
- Seal every brow in the parish, peasant and priest and sergeant alike, with your own thumbs dipped in your own blood.
- Challenge the Knight to single combat on Michael's footprint, and learn whose side the rock is on.

### The sixth bell: the Bell of the Four Winds

At the sixth bell the four angels bound at the great river are loosed, and here the great river is the Couesnon. The corners of the world come unbound. The north wind blows off the Channel smelling of ice and Englishmen. The west wind carries drowned church-music off the Atlantic. The south wind is hot and full of red dust, and the east wind is full of Norman curses. They meet over Mont-Dol, and the sky spins like water down a drain. The Couesnon goes mad again. It rears out of its bed like a hooked eel and carves new channels overnight, swallowing whole farms. The Mount is in Brittany, then in Normandy, then Brittany again, faster than any notary can write. The dikes burst one after another like siege engines, and the weirs are matchwood. Perrine stands on the boiling-house roof above six feet of brown flood, lantern high, roaring at God. The Merveille scaffolds collapse in one long shriek of timber, taking three monks with them. Rogier rings the bells of Dol against the storm, but the bells of Ys are louder, and they are coming closer. Lashed to the summit rock, the magi feel the hill turning on its root beneath them like a millstone.

- Bind the fifth wind, the one nobody names, into Aldric's body, and make him the new corner of the world.
- Break the last dikes yourselves and throw the country open to whatever is coming.
- Turn the maddened Couesnon toward Ys and let the river dig the city out.

### The seventh bell: and there was no more sea

The seventh bell is silence, half an hour of it in heaven and over the bay: no gull, no wave, no heartbeat. Then the tide goes out past Tombelaine, past Granville, past the edge of the world, and it doesn't come back. It never will. Forty miles of bare grey mud steam under a sun that is whole again, too white, like a drawn sword. Ys stands in the mud. It has towers of green-veined marble, streets of blue glass, and bells in every steeple still swinging and dripping and singing the seventh note. Its gates stand open. Its people, pale as shell, stand in the streets and bow to Mont-Dol. The monks of the Merveille cross the mud in procession behind a raised cross, weeping, and Guillaume trudges among them with his empty basket, a fisher of nothing. Aldric feels the sea's absence like a phantom limb, and every drop of water in his body strains to leave him. Sabine's heart keeps beating this time. Hervé finally laughs. On the hill, the Archangel's footprint fills with the last water in the world. In the heart of Ys an empty throne faces the hill.

- Walk down into Ys and take the throne.
- Ring the first bell backwards and call the sea home.
- Kneel on the footprint, all three of you, and let Michael finish the fight he started on this hill.

### Bonus ravings

- Every eel in Christendom has begun swimming toward Mont-Dol, some of them overland.
- Hamon's report to the bishop is written in a hand that isn't his, and in Breton.
- A calf born at Roz-sur-Couesnon has a bell for a heart, and you can hear it toll if you lay your ear to its ribs.
- The Merveille's master mason has carved an eighth bell into a cloister capital and won't say who told him to.
- At low tide the bog-oak stumps are sprouting fresh green acorns.
- Perrine swears there is one salt crystal in every sack shaped like a tiny key.
- Rogier baptised a child who walked out of the bay, and the water in the font turned salt.
- The Devil left claw marks on Mont-Dol too. Since the fifth bell there are more of them, and they are fresher.
